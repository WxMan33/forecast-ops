How signals are set
HIGH STP ≥ 1 or SCP ≥ 4.
ELEVATED SCP ≥ 1, or CAPE ≥ 1000 J/kg with 0–6 km shear ≥ 40 kt.
MARGINAL CAPE ≥ 500 J/kg with 0–6 km shear ≥ 25 kt.
LTD Limited data. Shown on top of any signal level when shear, SRH or LCL can't be computed for that hour, so the signal could be understated. Hover for what's missing.
LOW None of the above, or CAPE under 100 J/kg.
These are Heads-Up status colors used for signal strength in model guidance, not an instruction to act or an official outlook.
Ingredient flags
HAIL CAPE ≥ 1000, 700–500 hPa lapse rate ≥ 7 °C/km, 0–6 km shear ≥ 30 kt.
WIND CAPE ≥ 500, with a 0–3 km lapse rate ≥ 8 °C/km or a 0–6 km mean wind ≥ 35 kt.
TOR CAPE ≥ 250, 0–1 km SRH ≥ 100, LCL ≤ 1250 m, 0–6 km shear ≥ 30 kt.
A flag needs every one of its inputs. Missing data never raises or clears a flag.
How these are computed
Open-Meteo gives each model's CAPE, CIN and lifted index, plus winds, temperatures and heights on pressure levels. Shear, helicity, Bunkers motion, lapse rates and LCL are calculated here from those levels, about 50–150 hPa apart, and the 10 m wind. STP and SCP are the fixed-layer versions: STP uses 0–1 km SRH and 0–6 km shear, and SCP uses 0–3 km SRH and 0–6 km shear in place of the effective-layer values. Expect a rougher picture than SPC mesoanalysis or a full-resolution sounding. Which kind of CAPE each model reports varies.
